# 02 — Reconcile the vision before selecting its mechanisms

**Owner:** product/intent reviewers and coordinator, with a separate primary-source reader where available. **Output:** a reviewed working vision, sourced tradeoffs and the early review-method result. **Prerequisites:** 00 and sufficient 01 evidence to examine the significant goals; later factual reconstruction can continue in parallel. **Stop:** planning commitments and conditional choices, not new production truth.

Read [user-source excerpts](intent-source-excerpts.md), [context and preservation](context-and-preservation.md), [deliberation](deliberation-protocol.md), [whiteboard and decisions](whiteboard-and-decisions.md), [trial guide](review-method-trial.md), the current intent register and source dossier. Follow [documentation](../../practices/documentation.md), [implementation](../../practices/implementation.md) and [workflow](../../workflow.md) practices. Consult original user context where actually accessible, not invented memories.

## Decide what belongs in the product, not which Rust model wins

For each consequential seed outcome and newly recovered intention, separate:

1. **The problem and desired ability:** who needs it, what valuable work it enables and how its absence matters.
2. **The proposed explanation/mechanism:** a node type, workflow form, publication requirement, container choice, permission layout or particular screen.
3. **The current standing:** explicit instruction, exploration, outdated assumption, still useful but unproven aspiration, or an actual unresolved product choice.

Use the independent origin/standing/support fields; desirability and implementation state can coexist and differ. Do not treat all statements in `vision.md` as enduring purpose. Some may be accumulated design decisions. Nor should an available capability automatically become a user requirement.

Preserve valuable intent when revising its first explanation. A request to reuse work is not automatically a demand for a new method class. A wish for dependable adaptation is not automatically a demand for a static/dynamic mode switch. Standards familiarity is not measured model proficiency. These examples expose questions; they do not choose their answers.

## Reconcile conflicts explicitly

Build a concise account of consequential conflicts, each with sources, scope, plausible readings, current behavior and affected decisions. Determine whether it is:

- a real contradiction between desired outcomes;
- different scopes that can legitimately coexist;
- competing mechanisms for the same need;
- an older idea actually superseded by a later instruction;
- an agent interpretation wrongly promoted into a requirement;
- an absent capability, uncertain demand or unavailable evidence rather than a contradiction.

Do not resolve conflict by keeping every claim with vague “flexibility,” or by deleting the harder ability. Do not let two mutually inconsistent requirements survive as optional modes unless the user need and interactions justify those modes. Investigate a broader coherent interpretation when individual sentences are misleading, but identify it as your derivation and test it against original intent and neighboring outcomes.

Account for core value across the spectrum: human usability, inspectable agent-authored work, controlled changes during execution, useful reuse, independent owners, direct operations, managed tools/services, privacy and authority, recovery, evidence and evaluated improvement. V01–V12 are seeds to assess, not a list of mandatory representations. Missing ambitions must be surfaced; new ambitions are proposals, not automatic scope.

## Exercise the process while resolving the first difficult question

Run [the review-method trial](review-method-trial.md) on one real vision/product-model boundary, not after the entire target is selected. Use independent intake, genuine opposing grounds, the smallest discriminating evidence, a fresh-context reader and a separately labeled hypothetical premise change.

Feed any process failure back into the context/procedure before further decisions depend on the same error. The trial may favor retention or change; it must not be rigged to demonstrate that the coordinator is right. Its outcome becomes ordinary project reasoning, not a separate ritual report.

## Form a reviewed working baseline

Write `working/vision-baseline.md` as a concise derived view linking the relevant whiteboard decisions and primary grounds. It must explain:

- whom Milkdrift serves and the work it should make possible;
- the distinctive useful abilities being preserved or strengthened;
- agreed outcome commitments separated from proposed implementations;
- protections and tradeoffs, including what still needs the user's choice;
- superseded or disputed assumptions, with reasons rather than age alone;
- evidence and scenarios that would reveal a mistaken commitment;
- which downstream design choices are ready to compare and which remain conditional.

Do not produce another lengthy manifesto or rewrite canonical vision/status as though recommendations were approved. The baseline is a selected working hypothesis for planning, not a guarantee of product-market fit or implementation correctness. Each consequential conclusion links to the current decision/source version so a later summary cannot become its own proof.

The coordinator can recommend technical options and compose a coherent baseline within explicit goals. Removing a meaningful outcome, weakening protection or replacing a firm user instruction remains approval-required. State the exact choice and consequences. Do not repeatedly interrupt for preferences that do not block current work; continue independent investigations and preserve conditional branches.

## Cross-check before using the baseline

Ask a reviewer who did not write the synthesis to trace selected high-impact commitments back to original statements and actual source. Check both directions: an attractive plan must not lose an intended ability; an old mechanism must not survive only because someone mislabeled it a goal. Read the preservation map against the baseline and disclose differences.

Give a concrete success/failure story for each consequential commitment, without requiring the old product nouns to describe it. The method/reuse, autonomy/reliability and standalone multi-owner questions deserve deep review because many later decisions depend on them. Broader inventory completeness belongs in the dossier, not a new goal for every struct.

## Commit, handoff and gate

Commit the independently grounded conflict/intention account when coherent, then the reviewed baseline and actual trial corrections. Use documentation checks and any focused observation checks warranted by 01; no full runtime gate for prose-only planning.

Update the context index and handoff with the reviewed source/decision revisions, unresolved choices and their dependencies. **Gate:** 03 can derive product behavior from reviewed needs rather than scrambled historic mechanisms. Architecture can still inform and reopen these choices; it cannot silently reinterpret them. A known process failure or unresolved foundational preference is not hidden by declaring the vision finished.
